Internet Measured

An independent data-journalism studio. We research how online video works — what gets watched, what disappears, and why — and publish our findings as documentary videos on our YouTube channel.

Our research uses public data from YouTube API Services.

What we do

Each of our videos starts with a question about online video culture. To answer it, we use an internal research tool that reads public information through the YouTube Data API: video and channel statistics (view, like and comment counts), titles, publish dates, durations and the public most-popular charts. We study these numbers in aggregate and turn the results into documentary stories.

Our research tool is used only by our own small team. It has no public users, no accounts and no sign-up. It does not upload, edit, comment, subscribe or change anything on YouTube; all access is read-only.

How we label our numbers

Some figures in our videos — such as medians, shares, rankings and comparisons — are calculated independently by Internet Measured from public YouTube API data. We clearly label them as our own calculations. They are not metrics provided by YouTube.

How we handle data

  • We collect only publicly available information. We do not collect personal information about viewers or creators beyond what YouTube already makes public on a video or channel page.
  • We store public statistics only as long as YouTube's Developer Policies allow, and refresh or delete them when required.
  • We never sell, rent or share stored data with anyone.
  • Details are in our Privacy Policy. By using anything we publish that relies on YouTube API Services, you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service.
